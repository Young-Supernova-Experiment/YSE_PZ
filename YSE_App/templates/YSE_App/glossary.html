Real/bogus score

A machine-learning score from the survey or broker pipeline, from 0 to 1, for how likely a detection is a real astrophysical source (near 1) rather than an artefact such as a bad subtraction, a cosmic ray or a bad pixel (near 0).

The transient's real_bogus_score keeps the score from its ingest; for Pan-STARRS objects this is the QUB score, which also runs from 0 to 1. On Broker Candidates the RB column is the broker's score (ZTF rb, or the deep-learning drb when there is no rb). Each survey trains its own classifier, so compare scores within one survey.

Quality flags

Each photometry point and spectrum has a quality mask: a number in which every set bit records one problem. 0 is the best data. Higher bits are more serious, and REJECTED (bit 51) means "do not use". OTHER (bit 50) means "there is an issue; someone should look". NOT_PUBLISHED (bit 0) only says the data are not in a paper yet.

Older points may instead carry a named data-quality flag (for example "Bad Subtraction"). Points with one are left out of the latest magnitude and the Bazin fit.

MJD (Modified Julian Date)

A count of days: MJD = JD − 2400000.5, so MJD 0 is 1858-11-17 00:00 UTC and the day starts at midnight UTC. Fractions are fractions of a day. The site shows dates as UTC YYYYMMDD.ddd with the MJD when you hover over them.

Magnitude systems

Magnitudes are logarithmic and brighter is smaller: 5 magnitudes is a factor of 100 in flux.

Most data on the site are AB magnitudes. Each point stores its own magnitude system (mag_sys), and that stored system is the one shown with the point.

AB
Zero point set by a flat spectrum of 3631 Jy. Survey photometry (Pan-STARRS, ZTF, ATLAS, Rubin) is in AB. Fluxes stored by the ZTF and Rubin ingests use an AB zero point of 27.5.
Vega
Zero point set by the star Vega. The fits treat Bessell (UBVRI) bands as Vega magnitudes.
Upper limit
A non-detection: the source was fainter than this magnitude.

Phase

Time in days relative to a reference date. Always check which one:

Days since discovery
Today minus the discovery date (the follow-up and planning tables).
SALT3 phase
Days from t0, the fitted time of B-band maximum, in the rest frame (observer-frame days divided by 1 + z).
Spectrum phase
The phase stored with a spectrum when it was uploaded. It is usually relative to the time of maximum light; in some cases it is relative to discovery or to the time of first light. "—" means none was recorded.

Bazin vs SALT

Bazin
A simple rise-and-fall curve (Bazin et al. 2009) fitted to any transient's detections in all bands at once. It makes no assumption about the type, so it is the default for predicting tonight's magnitude (Show Bazin Fit, the Bazin Mag column). It describes the shape only and can be poor far from the data.
SALT (SALT3)
A model for Type Ia supernovae only. The fit gives t0 (time of maximum), redshift, stretch x1, colour c and peak magnitude mB. It is run on request (Refit on the Summary tab) and the stored fit is shown with Show SALT3 Fit. On other types its numbers mean little.
